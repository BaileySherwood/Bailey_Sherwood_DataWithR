Gestalt

Comments

Objective:
The objective of the image is to show that SpaceX overwhelmingly owns more operational satellites than any other organisation.

Preattentive Processing:
The first thing that grabbed my attention was the size of the SpaceX rocket, showing that SpaceX has launched substantially more satellites than any other organisation.

Gestalt Law:
The infographic uses the Gestalt Law of Proximity by placing all non-SpaceX satellite owners close together beside one rocket. This causes viewers to perceive them as a single comparison group, while SpaceX is isolated to emphasise its significantly larger satellite fleet.

Visual Comparison Accuracy:
The image mainly uses length to compare satellite ownership, with the height of the rocket representing the number of satellites.

Reference:
Visual Capitalist. (2026). Ranked: Who owns the most satellites? Voronoi.

Module 7 – Data Card
Title Australia’s Annual CO₂ Emissions by Source Over Time
Summary This multivariate visualisation shows Australia’s total annual CO₂ emissions and the amount produced by coal, oil, gas, cement, flaring and other industry. This allows the total emissions and their individual sources to be viewed together.
Data Sources

Original dataset:
Andrew, R. M., & Peters, G. P. (2025). The Global Carbon Project’s fossil CO₂ emissions dataset (Version 2025v15) [Data set]. Zenodo. View the original dataset

Processed database:
Rosado, P., Ritchie, H., Roser, M., Mathieu, E., & Macdonald, B. (n.d.). Data on CO₂ and greenhouse gas emissions [Data set]. Our World in Data. View the processed database

Variables Used Country, year, total CO₂ emissions, coal CO₂, oil CO₂, gas CO₂, cement CO₂, flaring CO₂ and other industry CO₂.
Mapping
  • Horizontal axis: Year.
  • Vertical axis: Annual CO₂ emissions measured in million tonnes per year.
  • Coloured areas: Emissions produced by each source.
  • Total line: Australia’s total annual fossil CO₂ emissions.
Important Notes
  • The dataset was filtered to include Australia only.
  • The values represent production-based territorial emissions.
  • Other greenhouse gases and emissions from land-use change are not included.
  • Historical values are estimates and may be revised.
Access The original data can be accessed through Zenodo. The processed CSV file and its codebook can be accessed through the Our World in Data GitHub repository.

Module 8

Description:
The aim of this module was to recreate and simplify the Australian land-use map. I first located the original data from ABARES and used Python to organise the agricultural land-use information. I then created an isarithmic-style map to show where agricultural land was most concentrated across Australia during 2015–16. After creating the first version, I changed the colours to a softer, colour-blind-friendly palette so the map would be easier and more comfortable to view. Overall, the final visualisation presents the original information in a clearer and simpler way.

Module 8 – Data Card
Title Agricultural Land Use Concentration Across Australia in 2015–16
Summary This isarithmic-style map shows the concentration of agricultural land use across Australia during 2015–16. The original land-use data was simplified and smoothed to show broader regional patterns.
Data Source Australian Bureau of Agricultural and Resource Economics and Sciences. (2022). Land use of Australia 2010–11 to 2015–16, 250 m [Data set]. Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry. View the original dataset
Variables Used Geographic location and agricultural land-use classification.
Mapping
  • Position: The geographic location within Australia.
  • Colour: The concentration of agricultural land use.
  • Lighter colours: Lower agricultural concentration.
  • Darker colours: Higher agricultural concentration.
Important Notes
  • The visualisation represents land use during 2015–16.
  • The original categorical data was simplified and smoothed.
  • The map shows broader concentration patterns rather than exact property or land-parcel boundaries.
  • A colour-blind-friendly palette was used to improve accessibility.
Access The original land-use dataset can be downloaded from the Australian Government dataset record using the link above.
